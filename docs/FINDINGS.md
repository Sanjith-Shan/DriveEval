## Finding (a): how much log-replay evaluation flatters the planner

Same planner, same configuration, same scenarios, two agent models. Paired over scenario id, bootstrapped over **scenarios** rather than timesteps, because 109 steps of one scenario are not 109 independent draws.

| metric | log-replay | reactive | absolute change | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `at_fault_collision` | 4.58% | 4.82% | 0.24% [-0.34, 0.85] pts | 1.05x [0.93, 1.20] |
| `collision` | 8.60% | 5.20% | -3.40% [-4.12, -2.69] pts | 0.60x [0.54, 0.67] |
| `drivable_area_violation` | 15.32% | 11.37% | -3.95% [-4.74, -3.15] pts | 0.74x [0.70, 0.79] |
| `comfort_violation` | 5.95% | 2.82% | -3.12% [-3.68, -2.56] pts | 0.47x [0.41, 0.54] |

Paired scenarios: 7,968.

**Null result on at-fault collisions.** The ratio is 1.05x with a 95% interval of [0.93, 1.20], which contains 1.0. This measurement does not establish that log-replay flatters the planner on the metric that actually scores the planner. Reported rather than discarded.

**But it does distort the headline collision rate, in the opposite direction to the usual assumption.** Total collisions run 1.65x *higher* under log-replay (95% CI [1.49, 1.85]). The mechanism is visible in the split below: log-replay agents ignore the ego, so they drive into it, and every one of those is a collision the planner did not cause.

| agent model | not-at-fault collision rate |
| --- | --- |
| log_replay | 4.02% |
| reactive | 0.38% |

So the honest statement is not that log-replay flatters a planner. It is that log-replay **manufactures collisions the planner is not responsible for**, and a harness that reports a bare collision rate under log-replay is measuring its own agent model as much as the policy.

Kendall tau between the two rankings: **-1.000** (p = 0.083, 4 configurations). The orderings disagree.

With this few configurations the p-value is weak, and it is printed so that weakness is visible rather than implied. What is not weak is the mechanism, which the table shows directly: the configuration with the **best** ADE is `pure_pursuit`, the controller with no obstacle reasoning at all, and it collides in over half of its scenarios. It scores well on ADE precisely *because* it does not react. The logged human never had to avoid anything, so the closest trajectory to theirs is the one that also does not avoid anything. Ranking planners by human-likeness does not merely differ from ranking them by safety here, it inverts it.

## Planning latency

Over 7,968 scenarios and 868,512 planning cycles, the median scenario's p50 cycle time is **0.98 ms** and its p99 is **3.51 ms**. Cycle-weighted mean 1.53 ms, worst single cycle 52.9 ms. The 10 Hz budget is 100 ms.

Heap allocations inside the planning cycle are not counted in a batch run: the counting `operator new` is linked only into `drive_bench` and the test binary, so a zero here would mean 'not measured' rather than 'measured and zero'. The measured figure is in the benchmark section and is asserted by `tests/test_no_alloc.cpp`, which also verifies that the counter itself works.

The pooled per-cycle p99 is NOT recoverable from per-scenario summaries: a percentile of percentiles is not a percentile. What is reported here is the distribution of per-scenario p99s, labelled as such. The median of the per-scenario p50s is the honest headline; the tail figures are OS scheduling noise as much as planner behaviour.
